How much of the county sits inside 1,000 feet

We counted 305,868 homes. A home is an address point on a housing lot.

State law alone puts 39.47% of those homes inside 1,000 feet of a school or licensed child care.

State law plus city rules in Cincinnati, Norwood, Reading, Golf Manor, and Evendale puts 41.62% inside 1,000 feet.

The leftover school-owned lots

Ohio also counts a school-owned lot if instruction, sports, or training actually happen there. We could confirm some of those lots. 2,180 other school-owned lots were not fully checked. 12 rented school sites were listed but not added.

How we measured

Each protected place starts at its lot, not a map pin. We grow a 1,000-foot zone from that lot line. We then ask which homes sit inside that zone.

A condo unit or a lot in a planned development is measured from the whole development: every unit lot plus the shared common land. That is the stricter reading. No Ohio court has decided it.

Child care counts only if the state lists it as an open center or preschool. In-home aides, type A/B family homes, and day camps are out. School-age programs are flagged and are not in the main number.

City rules add parks, pools, libraries, or rec centers in five places. Those zones stop at that city’s line.

Cincinnati extras are official rec centers and pools, not CRC playgrounds, sports fields, or boat ramps. A sports complex is not a recreation center under Ch. 763-5. Schmidt Recreation Complex is unshaded for that reason.
